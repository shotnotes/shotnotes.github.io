FAQ

I shoot with a Canon digital SLR or mirrorless camera. Do I need Shot Notes?

Short answer: No. However, there is more nuance to the answer, so read on.

Digital single lens reflex (SLR) and mirrorless cameras from Canon, Nikon, Sony, and other manufacturers have electronic contacts on their lens mounts that allow the cameras and the lens to communicate. The cameras can accurately determine most of the information that Shot Notes would let you record. Your digital image file contains complete and correct information.

However, if you use a manual lens (perhaps, through an adapter) that does not feature electronic contacts then Shot Notes can help you record the details about your shots and correctly associate them with your images.

Why 'determine most of the information' in the previous answer?

The purpose of Shot Notes is to capture your creative decision: the reasoning behind the shot, the lens you chose, the aperture, certain accessories relevant to the final image and so on. Thus, the information that Shot Note allows you to note is more than what a camera can determine on its own even with a fully electronic lens.

I shoot with a film camera. Do I need Shot Notes?

Yes, you can use it, but Shot Notes is designed for digital cameras and does not record information about film rolls, yet!

Is Shot Notes only useful if I use Leica M digital cameras?

No, Shot Notes can be used with any digital camera with lenses that do not have supported electronic contacts to communicate with the camera. More importantly, Shot Notes is designed to capture the creative decisions behind each shot, regardless of the camera or lens used.

What about the privacy of my information?

Shot Notes keeps your information on your own devices. There is no account, no analytics and no advertising. The phone’s location is read only when you tap the location button, and only to fill in the shot you are logging.

To lookup place names, the coordinates of the phone’s location are sent to Apple (on iOS) or Google (on Android). This behaviour can be turned off from the settings. The map button, when you tap it, hands a shot’s recorded location to a map app on your phone. Problem reports are sent only if you choose to, and show you what they contain first. The privacy policy has the whole picture.

When can I get it?

Shot Notes is in development and not yet in the app stores. Watch this space for updates.

Is Shot Notes free?

Recording (i.e., noting down information about) shots is free, always, and you can record as many as you like. Exporting is free for up to 24 shots a month. There is an optional subscription (see Pricing) that unlocks additional features, including unlimited exports.

What does it mean by 'exporting' shots?

Exporting shots means saving them in a format that can be utilised by the desktop or the command-line applications or the Lightroom plugin for Shot Notes to automatically update the metadata of your digital image file to reflect the information you have recorded.

What counts towards the 24 exports a month?

Each different shot you export counts once, however many times you export it. The month is the calendar month in your device’s time, so it starts again on the 1st.

What happens if I stop subscribing?

You go back to Free the following month. Nothing you have recorded is lost or locked, and you can still export up to 24 shots a month.

Do I need an account?

No. There is nothing to sign up for. A subscription uses the App Store or Google Play account already on your phone.

I shoot with a Leica M11-P that writes Content Credentials. Will Shot Notes handle them correctly?

Yes, that is specifically a case Shot Notes is designed for.

The M11-P signs each photo with Content Credentials, a record of where the photo came from. When a file has credentials, Shot Notes adds a new signed record on top of the existing ones. The image itself is not touched. This has been tried on Leica M11-P files.

Two things to know. The new record is signed by your credentials or credentials generated for you by Shot Notes. A software that checks credentials shows Leica’s signature as the original while the signature from Shot Notes may not be recognised depending on the certificate used. Before writing anything, Shot Notes shows which of your files have credentials, so you can choose to leave the files as is and keep the notes in separate sidecars files instead, or keep the new records in separate files next to the image files. Note however that Lightroom reads the lens name, keywords and description from a sidecar, but not the aperture or focal length of a DNG.

Could my original image files be corrupted by Shot Notes?

Shot Notes has been tested with DNG files and has not been found to corrupt them. Shot Notes also offers the option to write the metadata to a sidecar file instead of the image file itself. In addition, Shot Notes provides the option to keep backup copies of the image files that it edits, if you chose to write the metadata directly to the image files.
